# Design Direction

## Personal feel

- Warm, personal, quietly optimistic, and grounded.
- Let a love of plants and Zen show through in subtle botanical details, organic shapes, natural textures, and calm pacing.
- Present Kane as a thoughtful, technically grounded builder working across applied AI, data science, and machine-learning systems.
- Keep the language welcoming to anyone curious about the work while making experience and technical contributions legible to recruiters.

## Visual language

- Prefer a warm earth-tone palette: moss and forest greens, clay, sand, cream, and charcoal.
- Use an editorial, magazine-like composition with generous whitespace, strong typography, and relevant project imagery.
- Completed-project illustrations should be compact, uncropped thumbnails, not full-height panels. Use a contrasting pale slate surround and a light frame to separate earthy artwork from the text; leave active-project demos prominent.
- Use plant-inspired marks as accents, not wallpaper. Avoid loud gradients, excessive glass effects, or a generic corporate dashboard aesthetic.
- Favor subtle, purposeful transitions. Respect reduced-motion settings and keep the site comfortable on mobile.

## Content hierarchy

1. A clear personal introduction and routes to Projects and Experience.
2. MorningTide as a featured, active project, including its available demo video.
3. Current research: the steel-slag SLM capstone and the LLM-distillation bias benchmark.
4. A chronological, scannable portfolio and professional experience.
5. A personal story that connects curiosity, vitality, and technical work without sacrificing factual clarity.
6. Direct contact and professional profiles.

The preferred first impression is a humane, curious builder whose technical work is practical and carefully considered—not a list of tools or a claim-heavy sales pitch.
